# Use the native diagram viewer

This is the reference for every key and screen. To start, read the one-screen [quick start](quick-start.md).

The native renderer is the default for `pathlight show` and `pathlight view`. It is the only renderer and draws interactive nodes without images.

From the repository root, run this command in a terminal:

```sh
bun run pathlight view live --project pathlight
```

Click a node. Its border becomes white, and the details panel lists its incoming and outgoing relationships.
Connections are made of horizontal and vertical lines with rounded bends. Quiet ones are dotted, and the selected node's are solid: cyan for incoming, amber for outgoing.
The details use the same colors, node names, and full relationship labels. Scroll over the details to read wrapped labels without panning the diagram.
Click a node or use `h`, `j`, `k` and `l`, which select the nearest node left, down, up or right of the selection (siblings are preferred; ancestors and descendants are reached with Backspace and Enter). The camera only moves if the new node would be off screen. Arrow keys pan the camera without changing the selection, and Shift+`hjkl` pans too. Press `v` to swap the roles: arrows select, `hjkl` pan.
Enter goes into a box: into its children when this view draws them (a group, or the frame the view is about), else into the view it opens; `o` opens that view even when the children are drawn here, and the inspector says `o opens VIEW` only then. After Enter into a group, `b` or backspace reselects the group before anything else. When the view a box opens leaves smaller boxes out, the inspector says so under the counts (`4 smaller boxes are not drawn in its view`).
Press `b` or Backspace to return to the previous view, selection, zoom, and scroll position: the two keys are one stack. With nowhere to go back to, Backspace goes up instead: to the group that holds the selection in this view, else to the view whose box opens this one (that box selected); `b` says `No previous view`.
Press `f` to fit the diagram, `r` to center the selection, and `q` to quit.
Press `+` or `=` to zoom in, `-` to zoom out, and `0` to return to 100%.
Zoom keeps the viewport center in place. Keyboard pan, reveal, and zoom ease into position using a Three orthographic camera.
Hierarchy and search navigation reveal a group's upper-left corner or center a leaf.
Press `i` to toggle the inspector. From 120 columns up it is docked beside the diagram (drag its divider to resize it) and open by default. Under 120 columns with 40 rows or more (the pane beside Claude Code's transcript, a tall tmux split) it is docked below the diagram instead, a little under half the rows, the same head, cards and question line, and open by default; the diagram keeps the full width. Narrower, shorter terminals start without it; `i` (or a section number) shows it as an overlay on the right half, covering part of the diagram, and `i` or Esc hides it again. PageUp/PageDown scroll its contents. Press `?` for keyboard help: first what each connector color means, drawn in those colors, three to a row in the tiers' columns with a rule under them, then every key, in three tiers named for how far along you are with this codebase. **Understand it** (select with a click or a neighbor, `tab` through the inspector or a flow's steps, enter into a box, `o` the view a box opens, `b` and backspace back where you were or else up, `t` ask how it works or tell it what to change (one key, listed once), `F` step through a flow (from the talk, it turns the column to the node face; `i` goes back), `?`); **Direct it** (`c` what it did this session, and the review, `a` follow, `w` jump to the work, `e` files, `/` search (views, boxes, journeys, files; `tab` picks the kind), `g` views, `i` inspector or talk, `z` widen the talk, `f` `0` `+ -` `r` and the pan keys); **Shape the map** (`d` trace a flow, `m` improve the map, `n` name it yourself, `p` replay, `s` subagent calls, `T` themes, `1`–`4` and the keys inside a card, `v`, `q`). From 100 columns the tiers stand side by side, below that they stack. The tier you have reached (the highest one you have pressed a key of in this session) is in the accent color. The footer and empty states only ever name keys from the first tier.

The diagram is fitted and centered when it opens and whenever the terminal is resized, until you pan, zoom (`+`, `-`, `0`) or recenter (`r`) yourself; `f` fits it again and turns that back on. Switching views fits the new view. Fit zooms a small view in to fill the screen, but never above 150% (text does not grow with the cards), and it takes the connectors in as well as the cards.

A view is drawn with **compact fit** (`src/viewer/compact.ts`): LikeC4's arrangement (the order of cards on both axes, which cards share a rank, nesting, which side an edge leaves) with Pathlight's spacing and card sizes. Every card in a view is one size: the widest title decides it (on one or two lines, one when that costs at most two columns more; at least its longest word plus padding, from 20 to 32 columns), and the most lines any title takes at that width decide the height, so a short title has a blank row beneath it. The space between cards shrinks to 6 columns and 2 rows, more where group frames stand between them; a group is the box around its children with its frame and a clear cell. A view that still does not fit at 100% is fitted below it, with every card still one size: a card's cells come from its corner and its size, so zoom never changes one card's rows or columns without changing them all. A card's first row says what matters about it: a box that opens a view says how many boxes are inside it, the count in brackets (`[36]`, a package on the overview too), a leaf says nothing, and no card names its kind; while a session is watched, the same row holds the read and edit counts. The row is in every card of a view or none (a view whose cards differ in kind keeps it, blank on a leaf, so its layout does not change). The observed tag stays in the top border. When the zoom gives cards fewer rows than the layout did, that row goes from every card (before a line of a title), and a title that no longer fits ends in `…`.

A view opens on its entry: the subject of a neighborhood view, else the node the most connectors leave, else the first node that links to another view, else the first node.

Under 80 columns a diagram does not fit, so the nodes are listed instead: one row per node, indented by depth, with its kind (or its `[edit]`/`[read]`/`[run]` state while a tool runs) at the right. ↑↓ (or j k) move the selection down the list, Enter and Backspace go into a group and up to its parent, and clicking a row selects it. Search, ask, help and the inspector overlay work as usual.

Click an inspector section header once to focus it and again to fold it. Headers read `▾ 1 Connects ──── 4`: the number is the key that focuses the card (dim while it is folded, amber while it has focus).

Colors are 24-bit. When `COLORTERM` does not say `truecolor` or `24bit` (and `TERM` is not a direct-color type), every color is snapped to the nearest xterm-256 color; set `PATHLIGHT_COLORS=256` or `PATHLIGHT_COLORS=truecolor` to force either.

## Themes

Press `T` to choose a theme. The list previews each one live over the diagram as you move (up/down, PageUp/PageDown, Home/End); Enter keeps it and remembers it, Esc puts the previous one back. The choice is saved as `"theme": "name"` in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/pathlight/config.json` (default `~/.config/pathlight/config.json`), beside the Claude plugin's `"claude"` choice; nothing about any workspace is stored there. `PATHLIGHT_THEME=name` overrides it for one run.

Themes use [OpenCode's theme format](https://github.com/anomalyco/opencode), so its themes work unchanged: a JSON file with `defs` (named colors) and `theme` (roles mapped to a hex color, a `defs` name, another role, an ANSI color number, `transparent`, or `{ "dark": …, "light": … }`). Only the roles Pathlight draws with are read (`background`, `backgroundPanel`, `backgroundElement`, `text`, `textMuted`, `border`, `borderActive`, `borderSubtle`, `primary`, `accent`, `secondary`, `info`, `warning`, `success`, `error`); the diff, markdown and syntax roles are ignored. Pathlight's own shades (`grid`, `hot`, `pick`, `edgeDim`, `cardEdge`, `keycap`, `scrim`) are blended from those unless a theme sets them. `incoming` (live, search) is nudged away from `accent` (selection) when a theme gives them nearly the same color. A color with an alpha channel (`#rrggbbaa`) is laid over the theme's background.

- Built in: `pathlight` (the original look, the default) and the 32 themes bundled in `src/themes/` (copied from OpenCode, MIT, see `NOTICE.md`).
- Your own: put `name.json` in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/pathlight/themes/` or `<workspace>/.pathlight/themes/`. A workspace theme beats a user theme beats a bundled one of the same name. A file that does not parse is skipped; a theme that cannot be resolved (an unknown reference, a cycle) is refused with a message naming it.
- `system` takes the terminal's own background, foreground and ANSI colors. They are requested once at startup (OSC 4/10/11); the entry appears in the picker only if the terminal answers, and inside tmux that needs `set -g allow-passthrough on`. Verified only against fixed color values so far, not against a range of terminals.
- Light or dark: themes with both variants follow the terminal's background when it reports one; `PATHLIGHT_MODE=dark|light` forces it. Many themes define only one variant.
- With `COLORTERM` unset, colors are still snapped to xterm-256 for whichever theme is active.
Resize the terminal, then click another visible node to check that mouse targets stay aligned.

Press `/` to search everything: every view, every box (in any view, nested or not), every journey and every file the mapping gives a box, with the views and files outside the one you are in included. `tab` narrows the list to one kind and on round (all → views → boxes → journeys → files → all; `shift-tab` goes back): the picker's title names the chosen kind in the accent with the others dimmed beside it, so you see what the next `tab` gives. Choosing a journey reads it as `F` would, in the view that draws its steps apart. `F` still steps to the next journey. `g` opens the same list restricted to views.
Type to narrow it. Matching ignores case. Several words must all match, the letters of a word may be spread out (`ctx` finds `context`), and a swapped or doubled letter (`hepler`) still finds `helper`. A box matches on its name and its id, a view on its own name (the part of its title after the last ` / `, as the draft titles a view of something inside another `Holder / Own name`) and, a little weaker, on its whole title and its id, and a file on its name and its path. A view's row reads its own name first and the holder after it, dimmed and cut first, as a file's row reads its name and then its folder; its node count stays at the right. The picker's frame is at most 100 columns wide, and its right-hand column is as wide as its longest row needs, up to 26.
Results are ranked by how well the text matches and by where the result is, added together. Match quality counts most: an exact name or id comes first wherever it is, then a prefix or a word start, then a substring, then spread-out letters. Where it is breaks ties and nudges close calls, in this order: a box in the view you are in, a file in it, a box elsewhere, a journey, a view, a file elsewhere. A row for something in your view carries a dot (`● here`); a row elsewhere says which view it opens. Files the mapping ignores (tests, generated code) are not offered. The ranking constants and their tests are in `src/search.ts` and `tests/search.test.ts`.
Up, Down, `Ctrl+N`/`Ctrl+P`, PgUp, PgDn, Home and End move; Enter goes; Escape (or a click outside) closes without changing anything. A box in the view you are in is selected where it is. Otherwise Enter opens the view of its parent, else its own view, else a view it is linked from, else the smallest view that has it, selects the box, and Back returns. A file opens its box with that file marked in the Files section. A view just opens.
The list scrolls inside its frame (the border shows `79–95 of 95`). Typing, pasted text (256 characters at most) and resizing all work while it is open, `q`, `b` and `o` are text rather than commands, and diagram clicks do not change the selection. Ctrl+C still quits.

## Open a node's files

Set `VISUAL` or `EDITOR` before starting the viewer. The viewer prefers a nonempty `VISUAL` value.
For example, use `EDITOR=nvim bun run pathlight view live --project pathlight`.
For a GUI editor, include its wait flag, such as `VISUAL='code --wait'`.
These variables are trusted shell commands. Never populate them from untrusted model content.
When an agent starts the viewer, the viewer inherits that agent process's environment.

Select a node and press `e`. The inspector opens and scrolls to its file list; the heading becomes a path filter.
Type to filter paths. Use Up, Down, Tab, Shift+Tab, Page Up, or Page Down to select a file.
You can also click a visible file row to highlight it; click the highlighted row again, or press Enter, to open it in your editor. Clicking the diagram leaves file browsing and selects that node, so a stray click never traps the keyboard. Text in the panels is not mouse-selectable.
Exit the editor to return to the same file selection and diagram position.
Press Escape to return to diagram controls, or Ctrl+C to quit the viewer while browsing files.
While the editor runs, it owns terminal input and handles its own shortcuts.

The list uses direct mappings in `.pathlight/pathlight.activity.json`, including overlapping glob patterns.
It works without an activity session. A group does not inherit its children's files.
Mappings must name the selected project. Files are sorted and deduplicated, not inferred from recent activity.
Press Ctrl+R in the file list to find added, removed, or renamed files.
The mapping reloads automatically (see Live reload).

The scan includes hidden files but skips symlinks and directories named `.git`, `.cache`, or `node_modules`.
It does not apply `.gitignore` rules. Scans exceeding 50,000 directory entries report an error rather than a partial file list.
The viewer checks that a file still exists inside the workspace before opening it.
Missing editors, deleted files, and nonzero editor exits leave an error in the file-list footer.

## Explore groups and linked views

Try the nested Parcel architecture:

```sh
bun run pathlight view services --workspace examples/parcel --project parcel
```

Select the Parcel group, then the Commerce or Fulfillment group inside it. Click a child node to select it independently.
Use `view checkout` for a narrower diagram with two levels of groups.

To try navigation, start with `view index`. Select Parcel and press Enter to open services.
Select Commerce and press `o` to open checkout (Enter selects its first child). Press `b` to return to Commerce in services.
The viewer follows explicit LikeC4 `navigateTo` links. It does not guess a destination from shared elements.
History retains the latest 32 visits. Opening a missing or empty view leaves the current diagram intact.
Changing views replaces only the diagram widgets. The viewer process, activity binding, and activity polling stay running.

To observe an already-recording OpenCode session, supply its session ID:

```sh
bun run pathlight view live --project pathlight --session SESSION_ID
```

This command only reads activity. It does not enable recording in the agent plugin.
Edit a mapped file or run a command naming one in the bound session. The card carries a tag in its top border while the tool runs (`■ edit`, `▸ ⠋` for a command), then `✓ done` or `✗ failed` for 30 seconds, or `~ no result` for a call that started and never reported. A narrow card keeps only the glyph. Reads are not work: they are kept in the journal, `c` and replay, and shown nowhere live (no tag, no count, no Work row, no news, and follow and `w` never go to one).
Fast tools may show only their final state. White borders identify selection independently of activity labels and backgrounds.
Unselected borders pulse gently while a tool is active. Outcome states do not pulse. Connections do not animate or imply observed traffic.
Groups show their own mapped activity in their title. A child's activity does not automatically mark its parent active.

## Appearance

Leaf nodes are flat cards whose label wraps on words onto as many lines as the card has rows for and ends in `…` rather than showing a fragment. The ring is painted on the canvas just outside the card, only for selection (white), neighbors (cyan incoming, amber outgoing) and observed activity (amber edit, cyan read, violet command), so emphasis is never confused with decoration. Groups are outlines with titles on the canvas color.
Connections are orthogonal: thin box-drawing lines (`─ │ ╭ ╮ ╰ ╯ ├ ┤ ┬ ┴ ┼`) with rounded bends and no diagonals. Each is routed once per layout on a grid of whole terminal cells that keeps one clear cell from every card it does not enter, never travels along a group frame, and prefers few bends (`src/routes.ts`); it leaves the middle row of the source's right side and enters the middle row of the target's left side, at every zoom (the ends are taken from the cards as drawn). Connectors stop a cell short of the inspector's divider. Quiet connections are dotted where they run straight (`┈ ┊`), the selected node's are solid and tinted by direction, shared runs merge into junctions, and every arrowhead (`▶ ◀ ▲ ▼`) sits on the cell next to its node. What each color means is the first line of `?`: amber out of the selected box, cyan into it, dim dotted every other relationship, violet the agent's reading (a flow), and, while a diff is loaded, green added since the base and red dotted lost. Nothing on the diagram or in the footer says it.
A faint dot grid is fixed to the diagram's coordinates, so panning and zooming read as camera motion.
When the first view opens, nodes bloom in from left to right and connectors extend from source to target for about 0.6 seconds. This is a one-time reveal; it does not run on navigation and connectors never animate to suggest traffic. The ready signal waits for the reveal to finish.

To check how a change looks without a screenshot, render a pane to an image:

```sh
tmux capture-pane -e -p -N -t %PANE | python3 scripts/ansi2png.py out.png
```

The inspector shows the selected element's `kind`, `technology` and its LikeC4 `description` (markdown flattened to plain text, clipped at 420 characters), so the model is the one place that says what a component is for.
Each card carries a faint tint of its LikeC4 `color`, so databases, queues, services and externals read apart at a glance. Shapes are not drawn: edge treatments for storage and queues looked like stray fragments beside group outlines.

## Interface

The look is quiet: no bold, no all-caps, three text levels (bright for the selection and titles, normal for body, dim for everything else), and the only filled block on screen is the mode badge. The header reads `◆ pathlight / <view title>` (in journey mode followed by `◇ <journey> · 2/5 · <the step's box>` in the agent's color, and `← agent` when the agent's pointer put you there) with `● live` on the right (cyan; a spinner while a tool runs; the session's id is in `c`), and a dim second line with the box count and the Back depth. The right of that second line is the news line: what the agent and the session are doing, live only (see below).

Cards have one rounded border, a dim kind line (only when the view's cards are of more than one kind, or while a session is watched, since activity counts appear on that row; on every card of the view or on none) and the title directly beneath it, with the live tag set into the top border; the selected card's border is amber, its neighbors' borders are a step brighter, and the card chosen by the Connections cursor gets a dashed amber border. Connectors are thin orthogonal lines: each leaves the vertical middle of the source's right side, runs horizontally, turns vertically at the middle of the gap (edges leaving the same node share that trunk) and enters the vertical middle of the target's left side, with rounded corners and an arrowhead. The selected node's edges are solid amber (outgoing) or cyan (incoming); all others are dim and dotted. Cards are kept inside their group's frame so group titles stay readable at low zoom.

The bottom row is the only status line: an amber mode badge in lowercase (`diagram`, `connections`, `files`, `search`…), `Selected: <id>`, and, at the right, `zoom N%  │  ? help` (the version is in help). Key hints appear only inside a mode that needs them (section focus, search, files); in the diagram the footer stays quiet and `?` lists the keys. The header's second row is `N boxes` (plus `back N` once there is history) with the news line at its right, the one place for what the agent is doing: `following <box>  a stop` while follow is on, else `● working in VIEW` beside a highlighted `w` (“go and follow”) when the newest work is in another view and the session's turn is still open, else the call running now. The offer does not come back for work you already went to with `w` (or follow took you to) and came back from: only a later call in a different box, or a new turn, brings it back. The news line is shortened to fit and dropped on very narrow terminals. The footer answers what you pressed and never reports what the agent did: notices (`No previous view`, `Model reloaded`, errors) replace the hints for about two seconds; the ask confirmation stays for about three and a half. Help (`?`), the session summary (`c`), naming a box (`n`), go-to-view (`g`) and the theme list (`T`) share one frame: a single thin line in the dim edge color, centered in the terminal over a backdrop dimmed to about a fifth, one column of padding left and right and none above or below, as tall as its content and at most 100 columns wide, with the title (and, at the right, a status such as `in diagram` or the live session) set into the top border and the key hints set into the bottom border. Clicking the backdrop or pressing Esc closes a dialog, and every other key is ignored while one is open. In help, `v` swaps hjkl and the arrows; the tier you have reached is in the accent color. Empty states are one dim line saying what to do next.
A one-line `loading the architecture…` shows while LikeC4 loads the model.

The inspector ranks what it shows. At the top, the component's name (with `agent` at the right when the agent wrote its name and note, while it fits; no kind word), then what the model says it is for in the body color, then at most one dim caveat from the scan, only when it read fewer of the box's files than `e` lists (`6 of 14 files read by the scan`, or `5 Go files not read by the scan`); no file or line counts (and, in a dim line, its technology, how many smaller boxes the view it opens leaves out, and `o opens <view>` drawn as an action, `o` in the body color). Under that come cards with hairline headers (`▾ 1 Work ───── count`), most important first. Each header carries the number that focuses it: its place among the cards shown, so with no session Connects is `1`, and Files, hidden until asked for, is always the last number.

- **Now**: the selected component's own recent calls, newest first, up to three rows (`✓ read  pay.ts  ×2  4m`; repeats collapse), and `N earlier · c session` when there are more. It is shown only when a session is attached, and says `nothing observed here yet` rather than anything that could contradict the count. Work on other components is the header's line, `w`, `a` and `c`.
- **Connects**: one row per relationship, who calls it first (`← Login page  sends credentials`), then what it calls (`→`). The row under the cursor shows its relationship in full, wrapped.
- **Files**: shown only while you ask for it (`e`, or its number), as a nested tree; files touched this session are bright with an amber `●`.

Pinned under the cards, always the same two lines: what is one key away (`e files 6   c session   p replay`, each only when it applies) and the question field (`› how does Auth service work?  t`), which opens the talk or the modal with that question suggested: it is ghost text in the empty field with the field's glyph `→` standing before it (the key that takes it, as in Claude Code; no hint names it), typing replaces it, and Enter on an empty field sends nothing. For a journey (`d`) the suggestion comes from the box's own arrows: `how does <the first box with an arrow into it> reach <box>?`, else `what happens when <box> starts?`; improving the map suggests nothing. With nothing selected, the head says `No selection` and, under it, what the app is: the note of the box the view is about, else of the model's first top-level box. (`pathlight` refuses a view with no nodes, so today this is only reachable inside the viewer.)

Press `1`–`4` to focus the card with that number (its header turns amber and the mode badge names it); the movement keys belong to it until `Esc` or `Tab` takes them back (or the same number again). `h j k l` and the arrows never cross between the diagram and the inspector: from the right-most node `l` says there is nothing to the right, and in a card left and right do nothing. Inside a focused card `↑↓`/`jk` move a cursor (in Now they scroll) and `Enter` acts: in Connects it selects that node in the diagram (and the focus stays, so you can walk the graph edge by edge) and in Files it opens the file in your editor. `Tab` moves through what is on screen: from the diagram into the first card (`Shift+Tab` the last), then card by card, and past the last card (or before the first) back to the diagram; it never selects another box. In a flow, `Tab` and `Shift+Tab` step forward and back like `]` and `[`, from the diagram or a card. `Space` folds the focused card; clicking a header folds it and focuses it. Clicking the diagram hands the keys back.
Cards are sized to their content, capped at a fair share of the panel; only the focused card scrolls, with a thin scrollbar that is visible only while it is focused. When the inspector is too short for every open card, the least important untouched ones fold themselves.
Press `g` for a list of every view in the project. Type to narrow it (fuzzy: the letters of each word in order, so `hlp mid` finds `helper / middleware`), `↑`/`↓` or `Ctrl+N`/`Ctrl+P` choose, `PgUp`/`PgDn`/`Home`/`End` move further, Enter goes and `Back` returns. The list scrolls inside its frame and the border shows which rows (`79–95 of 95`) you are looking at. This is a user's choice of view, not an inferred link.

## Inside Claude Code (pane mode)

Experimental, off by default: `/pathlight:open` opens the tmux viewer beside the conversation, and `/pathlight:open pane [VIEW]` (for one command) or `pathlight config set claude pane` (kept as `"claude"` in `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/pathlight/config.json`, beside the theme; `tmux` puts it back, and the word in the command wins over the file) draws the viewer in Claude Code's own pane instead (`pathlight pane`, the same viewer on OpenTUI's headless renderer, its frames shown by the Claude plugin): in the fullscreen layout from 110 columns docked on the right of the transcript; on the main screen inline on top, above the prompt box, the full width and up to 45 rows, refused for the tmux pane (with a toast) when that leaves under 80 × 24. Claude Code places a mod's pane, so the layout (`/tui`) is what chooses between them; on the main screen, "on the right" is the tmux pane. It is off by default because Claude Code caps a docked pane at 45% of the terminal and offers no way to resize or place it (2026-11-01). Everything above holds there, with these differences, which apply only in the pane:

- **The agent's `pathlight show`.** A Bash call that is just `pathlight show VIEW` or `pathlight open [VIEW]` (in this workspace, no pipes or chains) opens this pane bound to the session, with that view, and is answered `Pathlight is beside the conversation.`; where the pane cannot be placed, or tmux is chosen, the command runs as it would.
- **Keys.** The pane opens with the keys and passes typed characters, Enter and Backspace at once, through the field at the left of its last row, which empties after every key (what you type is Pathlight's keys, not text kept there). Tab passes through the field too (Shift+Tab arrives as Tab: Claude Code's focus ring does not say which it was; `[` steps a flow back). The arrows, PgUp/PgDn, Home/End, Delete and ctrl chords reach the viewer after one click on the pane's last row, right of the field (the row says `click here`; a click on the field itself gives the keys to the field). The arrows then pan the diagram (or select, after `v`). Esc never reaches the viewer: Claude Code uses it to hand the keys back to the conversation; the pane stays, and a field you were typing in (the talk, search, naming, the file filter) is left as Esc would leave it.
- **Esc from the key row.** Claude Code tells the plugin nothing when the last row's Client gives the keys back (it reads the pane as unfocused from the click on), so Esc there leaves a field the viewer was typing in (search, the talk, naming, the file filter) as it was; Esc from the field leaves it as Esc would. Type into the field again, or press `q` outside a field, to leave the mode.
- **Back into the pane, and its size.** After Esc, `ctrl+x tab` gives the keys back to the pane's field (a click on the last row does too). tmux's own pane keys (`ctrl-h/j/k/l` and the like) are tmux's and do not enter the pane: it is Claude Code's, not a tmux pane. The dock opens at its widest, 45% of the terminal (Claude Code's ceiling); `ctrl+x x` closes it. Claude Code widens and narrows a pane with `ctrl+x ←` and `ctrl+x →` only while the pane holds the keys outside a field, which Pathlight's pane does not (its field keeps Tab for Pathlight), and no mouse drag resizes it (measured on 2.1.316).
- **`q` is Esc** wherever no field is typing: it leaves a focused card, a flow, the talk (a widened talk narrows first), the inspector drawn over the diagram, help, the session summary and the theme list. It never quits; the pane's ✕ closes it. In a field, `q` is a letter. Every hint that says `esc` says `q`, except in a field.
- **No pointer on the diagram.** Clicks over the diagram do not reach the viewer (Claude Code gives the keys to the conversation instead), so a box is selected with the keys or `/`.
- **The talk is a modal at every width.** In Claude Code's pane the conversation is beside the viewer, so `t`, `m` and `d` open the modal (the kinds of request as a tab bar with a rule under it, `▸ about <box>`, and a five-row field, growing to eight, with the suggested question) rather than the column. Tab changes the kind, `→` takes the suggestion, Enter sends the request with the box's files and connections and closes the modal, Esc closes it; the footer says what Claude Code answered (`Sent as your prompt`, queued, or refused).
- **The talk's request** goes up to Claude Code and is submitted as your prompt, the text byte for byte what would be typed into the agent's tmux pane; one sent during a running turn waits for it, and the talk says it is queued. One a `UserPromptSubmit` hook stops is said at once (`Stopped by a hook:` and the hook's reason).
- **No editor.** The pane has no terminal to hand to an editor, so Enter on a file (and `e` in a flow, and reading a review item) says in the footer that files open from the tmux viewer (`pathlight show`), or ask the agent.
- **Size.** Docked, the pane is at most 45% of the terminal's width: about 103 columns on a 232-column terminal (the inspector is below the diagram), 139 at 312 (docked beside it). Inline on the main screen it is the terminal's width less four, 37 rows on a 50-row terminal (the inspector beside the diagram from 120 columns).

## Live reload

The viewer watches the workspace for changes to `.c4`/`.likec4` files, LikeC4 configuration and `.pathlight/pathlight.activity.json`, and rebuilds the diagram about half a second after the last change. Your selection, pan and zoom stay where they are, and a short notice lists added and removed nodes (`Model reloaded: + Billing service`).
If the new model has errors (for example an agent is mid-edit), the viewer keeps the last good diagram and says so; it recovers on the next valid save. A new or removed view you are looking at is reported rather than dropped. Mapped files refresh with the mapping. `node_modules`, `.git` and `.cache` are ignored.

## Follow mode and the live display

`--follow` binds to the newest recorded session in the workspace and re-binds when another session writes later.
Until a session records, the header reads `live waiting for a session`.
The header shows a spinner while a tool runs. Its activity line is live only: `now` while a tool runs, what it does and in which component (`now ■ edit in Auth service`, with a subagent's label after the verb), and nothing once the call has an outcome; the files are in the Work card and the session summary (`c`). The Work card lists the selected component's own edits and commands, one short line each: a status glyph (spinner while running, `✓` done, `✗` failed, `~` no result), `edit`/`run`, the file name (`+N` more files, `×N` repeats) and a coarse age. Reads, and calls that name no file (a subagent launch, a search), are not listed; they stay in the journal, in `c` and in replay. Edits to files no component claims, which show where the mapping has a gap, are in the session summary (`c`) under `not in the model`; files the mapping lists under `ignore` are never shown, as they are deliberately outside the model. In the file list, files touched in the retained journal are bright with an amber `●`.
Nodes show `r` and `e` counts for retained reads and edits (groups append them to the title). Counts describe the retained journal, not totals.
Nodes that saw work in the last 90 seconds carry a faint tint (warm for edits, cool for reads) that cools in three coarse steps, so you can see where the agent has been.
Shell commands (`Bash`) are tracked too: the journal keeps only the existing workspace files the command names, never its text. A running command shows `[run]` on those nodes and counts as `c`. That means "a command referred to this file", not that it read or edited it.
Press `w` to select the node the agent is working on (the newest observed call that maps to a component; if it is not in this view, `w` offers the view that has it). Press `c` for a summary of the session: components touched with read, edit and command counts and recency, and the files edited with their owning component. Both are computed from the retained journal and make no claim beyond what was observed.

`p` replays the bound session from its retained journal. Each step is one call, shown as it ended (a call that never finished stays `started`). The diagram, counts and activity list show the session as it stood after that step, the newest call flashes, and the camera follows its component, switching view when the component is only in another one. Steps are grouped into turns: a turn begins at the first call after a prompt (Claude's `UserPromptSubmit` hook and OpenCode's session run write a bare marker, never the prompt), or, in a journal without markers, after a pause of more than 90 seconds. `←` `→` step, `[` `]` move between turns, `Home` `End` jump, `space` plays and pauses (stopping at the end), `s` cycles 1x, 2x and 4x, `esc` or `p` returns to live. Live updates are held back while replaying; zoom, pan, search and `?` still work.
Press `t` to ask the agent about the selected node; see [The talk](#the-talk).

### Behavior views

A state diagram an agent wrote (see the behavior skill, `/pathlight:behavior`) is an ordinary LikeC4 view, in a `*.behavior.c4` file beside the generated model. A state tagged `#bug` has a red outline and one tagged `#fixed` a green one (the tag also shows beside its kind in the inspector); selection and live activity still take precedence. Arrows carry no text, so the relationship titles (the events) are in the inspector under Connections. These views are the agent's reading of the code, not a scan. `pathlight flows` checks only what can be shown wrong or old: files a state names that no longer exist or changed since the commit in the view's `Synced at` note, arrows with no event, decisions with fewer than two answers, states nothing reaches, and states that name no file. A clean report is not a claim that the flow is right.

### Flow mode

A flow is a reading of the code that pathlight draws on the architecture, never an observation. There are two kinds, both found in the workspace on their own: a **journey** (a LikeC4 `dynamic view`: the components a request passes through, in order, each hop titled) and a **state diagram** an agent wrote in a `*.behavior.c4` file (its states are placed on components through the files their descriptions name and the activity mapping). They are read in the background after the first frame and again when the model changes or a check finishes, so a view never waits for them.

When the selected component is in a journey, an **In journeys** card (numbered after Connects) lists which journeys and which steps, with `◇ reading` at its right. Nothing is shown for a component no journey touches. (On screen everything is a journey; the CLI keeps `pathlight flows` and the files `*.behavior.c4`.) While one is read, the header's first line says which journey, which step and the step's own box, and the footer names only the keys (`[ ] step  F next journey  esc exit`). Press `F` to trace one (the flows through the selection first; `F` again goes to the next, and after the last it leaves; `esc` leaves too). A flow is read in the view that draws its steps apart: of the views that draw both ends of every hop (all of them, when none does), the one with the most distinct boxes for its steps; ties go to the view holding the selection, then to the deepest. When that is not the view you are in, the viewer goes there (the header's title says where; `b` comes back). After `t` with a how-question (or `d`), the first journey that appears in the workspace is announced (`Drawn: How plugins plug into client and server · F walks it`) and is what the next `F` opens, whatever is selected; its arrival moves neither the camera nor the selection:

A journey step that names the function it calls (`--step 'a -> b: event @ file#function'`) shows it in the step reader under the current step: the name, the file, and the declaration as the file writes it (no inferred types), wrapped to the panel. `e` opens that function in `VISUAL`/`EDITOR` at the line the file declares it on now (the line is passed to the vi family, nano, emacs, micro, kakoune and helix; any other editor opens the file at the top), and the viewer returns to the same step. `pathlight flow add` refuses a function the file does not export, and `pathlight flows` reports a call whose declaration has since changed or gone.

- Cards on the path carry their step number in the top border, on the left (`╭─ 2 ───╮`; `▶` marks where a journey starts; a component several steps reach shows `2·5`), in the violet that means the agent's reading. A journey's connectors between the steps' components are violet too, and a hop no connector draws gets its gap drawn as a dotted violet run along the route a connector would take (the agent's reading, present only for the flow being read). A hop between components inside a box this view draws lands on that box; a hop whose ends are in one box is its number only. Cards off the path step back (dimmed); entering flow mode keeps the camera where it is, and a fitted view never moves for a step.
- The inspector's Connects card becomes **Steps**, taking its place and number: `◇ a reading of the code, not observed`, when the flow was synced and whether the files it names changed since, then each step: the event across the panel (the step being read shows it in full) and, for a journey, the hop under it on its own lines, `<from> → <to>` in the muted tone behind a bar in the journey's violet, wrapped inside the bar and never clipped. Left of the arrow is the box whose code acts, right of the arrow is the box it reaches; the diagram's number badges and the walk's pointer follow the arrival (to), the ▶ marks where the journey begins. A state diagram's step shows its component beside the event when both fit and on the next row when not. `●` at the right of a row says the agent touched that component's files (from the journal, not that the step happened). The current step also shows the latest call on its component, `over http` (or `websocket`, `process`) when the agent recorded the hop as one made at run time rather than by an import (`--step 'a -> b over http: event'`; it is not reported as a gap), `≠ no arrow on the map for this hop` when the model has no relationship for any other journey hop (a hop back along the way an earlier step went shows `↩ the answer to an earlier step` instead, and `pathlight flows` reports several hops over one pair once), and `✗ marked as broken in the diagram` / `✓ marked as fixed` for a state tagged `#bug` / `#fixed`. `N to check · pathlight flows` appears when the check has findings.
- `[` and `]` (or `Tab` and `Shift+Tab`, or `↑` `↓` in the focused card) step; each step selects its component, moving the camera only when part of its card is off screen (never while the view is fitted), and a step whose box this view does not draw goes to the view that draws it and selects it there (`b` comes back). Choosing a component yourself moves the reader to that component's next step. The footer says `flow` and the position (`◇ Placing an order · 3/5`).

Observed activity and the flow are kept apart on purpose: the flow never changes what the journal shows, and a step is never marked passed or failed because a command ran. `pathlight flows` reports the same findings on the command line, and `pathlight see VIEW --keys F,]` prints this mode as text.

### The talk

Where the conversation is beside the viewer (a tmux split beside the agent's pane, Claude Code's pane), `t`, `m` and `d` open a modal: one request with the box's context, sent as the prompt, the reply in the conversation you can already see. Where the conversation is hidden, which in tmux is the viewer's window zoomed (`prefix z`, read on the resize a zoom causes), the inspector's column has two faces: the node (everything above) and the talk, the conversation with the agent beside the diagram, and `t`, `m` and `d` turn the column to the talk and put the keys in its field. Zooming or unzooming with a request open carries it across, its kind and its text (a column talk already sent closes when the conversation comes back into view). `pathlight view` without a pane to type into refuses to ask, as before. In the column, `i` flips between the faces and keeps the talk; selecting another box flips nothing (the talk's first line names it as the subject of the next request).

- **What it shows.** The bound session's conversation (the session the header's `live` names, bound the same way as activity): what you typed and the agent's text, in order, each under `you · 12:04` (in the accent color, your words one column in) or `agent · 12:04` (in the agent's color, the one journeys use), and `⠋ working` under your last request until the agent's text arrives, the newest at the bottom (the view stays there unless you scroll up with the wheel, or PageUp/PageDown in the field). Only the last 200 messages are kept. Tool calls, their results, thinking, a subagent's turns, slash commands and the host's own notices are not shown. The text is rendered lightly: paragraphs wrapped, list items kept, fenced code indented on the darker ground, `#` headings bold, nothing else interpreted. A request the viewer sent shows as the question you typed, without its context block. The conversation is read from the host's own file (Claude Code's transcript, which every hook names; OpenCode's database) while the talk has been opened, held in memory and never written anywhere: the hooks record only where the file is, whether the agent waits for you, and when its turn ended (`.cache/pathlight/sessions/`). With no session bound it says `no session bound yet · the reply lands in the agent pane`; a file that cannot be read says `cannot read the conversation`.
- **The state line**, at the right of the talk's first line: `working` (spinner) while a turn is open or a tool call is newer than its end, `waiting for you in its pane` while the agent shows a permission prompt or asks a question there (answer it in that pane: the talk cannot), `done` for ten minutes after the turn ended, then nothing. On the node face, a reply that arrives while the talk is not showing puts the one word `replied` at the right of the first line, and nothing else happens.
- **Sending.** Until the first request of a talk is sent, the composer is one block under the conversation: a rule, three tabs (`Ask`, `Improve the map`, `Trace a journey`; the chosen one in the text color, the others muted) and a row under them, then a rule, the field and its keys. Tab and Shift+Tab (or a click) change tab and keep what you typed. On `Ask` the row under the tabs says whom the question is about (`▸ about Backend server`, the title in the body color), with ` · records a journey` after it while the question will be drawn; on the other tabs it is one line on what the agent will do. The empty field shows the suggested question as ghost text and `→` takes it; Enter sends, Alt+Enter adds a line, an empty field sends nothing. The first request is typed into the agent's pane exactly as below (the same text `t`, `m` and `d` have always sent); every later one is your text alone, followed by Enter. After a send the keys go back to the diagram and the talk stays showing; `t` puts them in the field again (same talk, no tabs). `m` or `d` begin a new talk, as `t` does once the talk is closed. The field is three rows, growing to a third of the column, then scrolling.
- **Widths.** From 120 columns the talk is docked like the inspector, two fifths of the terminal between 44 and 64 columns, and the diagram refits while it is fitted; below that it lies over the right of the diagram at that width; under 80 columns it takes the whole body, as the node list does. `z` widens it to all but a strip of diagram (at most 120 columns) and `z` again restores it. For a full screen, zoom the viewer's tmux pane (`prefix z`).
- **Esc** steps back one level: from the field the keys go back to the diagram; then a widened talk narrows; then the talk closes and the node face returns. A click on the diagram also hands the keys back. Nothing the talk shows ever moves the camera or the selection.

`pathlight see VIEW --agent --keys t` draws the modal beside a stand-in agent pane (`cat` in `see`'s private tmux server); `--zoom` puts the viewer in a split beside it and zooms it, so `--zoom --keys t` draws the talk column.

### Asking or telling: `t`

`t` is one key for both. The talk types one line into the agent's pane: the question, the component's context (the files on its edges, the ones that import from or are imported by its neighbors' files, most such imports first, then the entry file its description names, at most six; and its connections; never the activity), then two rules. A question whose answer is a path through components is answered in chat **and** recorded as a journey with `pathlight flow add`, so it appears on the map and `F` walks it: the row under the tabs says ` · records a journey` while the question starts like a how-question (`how`, `where does`, `what happens`, `trace`, `walk me through`, `follow`); `d` is the way to ask for a journey explicitly. The map skill (`/pathlight:map`) tells the conversation to hand that recording to its `pathlight-tracer` agent when it has one; the line itself never says so, because a tracer handed it would read the sentence as its own. An instruction (anything that will edit files) is declared before the first edit: the agent runs `pathlight plan` with the box you pointed at and whatever else the change will reach, and adds anything new as a planned element, so the outline and a dashed box appear where you pointed before the work lights them. The instruction case needs nothing chosen.

### Changing the map from the viewer

Two more keys on the selected box, each with its own meaning. They need a map made by `pathlight draft`, which records each element's overlay key in `.cache/pathlight/keys.json`; on a model you wrote the viewer says so and does nothing.
- `m` asks the agent to improve the map: the talk on its `Improve the map` tab. The request typed into the agent's pane carries the overlay key, files and connections, and the rules: read the files and answer in chat; a rename, merge or split, or anything it advises against what you asked, it proposes with its reasons and waits for your yes, renaming and redrawing nothing until then; it never changes a `"by":"human"` entry. Use it for a box such as "loose files" whose purpose is not obvious.
- `d` asks the agent to trace a journey: the request on its `Trace a journey` tab. The request typed into the agent's pane carries the component's files and connections and the rules: follow the pathlight behavior skill, confirm each hop in a file, record it with `pathlight flow add` (which checks the components against the model and stamps the commit; a hop confirmed over HTTP, a websocket or a process is written `over http`), leave out any hop it cannot point to in the code, and change no product code. The request is the tracer's brief: the map skill tells the conversation to hand it to the `pathlight-tracer` agent the Claude plugin ships (pinned to a mid-size model, so the reading stays out of the conversation and off a larger one). Nothing appears until the agent has written the journey; then `F` traces it. With no journeys yet, `F` says to press `t` and ask how the selected box works.
- `n` is your own edit, with no agent, in a modal: a name, Enter, a note, Enter (a three-row field). The viewer writes `.pathlight/pathlight.overlay.json` with `"by":"human"` (a pin) and reruns the draft; the model reloads on its own. A name left as the generated one is not stored, so the box keeps following its folder; leaving both empty removes your entry.
This needs the viewer to have been opened with `pathlight show` (which records the parent pane); text is sent literally and the pane is checked to still exist.
Press `a` to follow the work: while a tool runs, the viewer selects the node it belongs to and glides the camera there; when this view does not draw that component but another view does, it goes to that view (the one `w` would choose) and `b` comes back; while follow is on the news line says `following <box>  a stop` (no toast); a component no view draws falls back to the nearest enclosing component in this view. The inspector shows that component's activity and files. It is off until you press `a`. Selecting something yourself, or panning or zooming, pauses it ("Follow paused · a to resume"); `a` again resumes, and once more turns it off. Follow and the offer go only to work (edits and commands), never to a read. When the newest work is in a component this view does not show and the agent's turn is open, the header offers "working in VIEW" beside a highlighted `w` ("go and follow"), once: after `w` and Back it stays away until a later call in a different box or a new turn; pressing `w` goes to the view that has it and turns follow on, so the camera keeps going where the agent works (`a` stops it) (one this view links to via `navigateTo` first, else the smallest containing view) and selects the component, and Back returns. When the inspector is short, the Work card is the last to fold.

An element the mapping lists under `planned` that has no files yet (nor anything inside it) is designed but not built: its border is dashed, its box carries a `[planned]` tag, the header counts the planned elements in the view (`3 boxes · 1 planned`), and the inspector says "planned: no files yet" under its title. It looks like any other element as soon as it has files. `pathlight check` reports the same plan.

A view written by `pathlight draft` for one component (`<component>_near`, reached with enter on that component) opens with that component selected, so the picture says what it is about.

Calls made inside a Claude subagent carry its type and show it after the file name in the Work card (`·Explore`) and in the header line (`[Explore]`). Press `s` to hide them and leave only the conversation's own calls, and again to show them; the choice lasts for the viewer's run and applies to replay too.

`pathlight view VIEW --select ELEMENT` opens with that element selected, and `pathlight see` prints the screen as text (see the README); the tests compare it with the snapshots in `tests/golden/`.

## Out-of-date badge

Every `pathlight check` records its counts in `.cache/pathlight/stale.json`, and the Claude plugin's `Stop` hook runs `pathlight check --quiet` at the end of each agent turn. The viewer reads that file every few seconds (it never scans itself) and, when the model and the code disagree, shows `▲ out of date: 3 files not in the model · 2 imports no relationship covers · checked 4m ago` at the right of the heading (shortened to `▲ out of date` in a narrow pane). The counts are `check`'s: source files no mapping claims, imports between mapped elements with no relationship, relationships no import backs, and mappings matching no file. They are static evidence that the two disagree, not which one is wrong, and they are as old as the last check: edits since then are not counted until the next turn ends or `pathlight check` runs. No stamp shows nothing; a clean one shows a muted `✓ in sync` (the age is shown only when something is out of date), so you can tell the hook is running. When most of the source is in languages the scan does not read (the stamp counts both), it says `✓ in sync · TS/JS only`, shortened to `✓ TS/JS only` before it is dropped. When the check cannot run because the model or the mapping is broken (it does not compile, or a mapping names a box that is gone), that is recorded too and the badge says `▲ out of date: the model can't be read · checked 2m ago` instead of keeping the last good result; the next check that runs clears it. The viewer itself keeps drawing the last good diagram and says the model has errors.

## Changes since a commit

`pathlight diff [--base REV]` (default `HEAD`) compares the relationships the imports support in the working tree with those at a git commit, using the current model and mapping on both, and records the result in `.cache/pathlight/diff.json` (the `Stop` hook runs it after `check`). The base commit's scan is kept in `.cache/pathlight/base/<commit>.json` (only the latest commit) so later runs skip unpacking and scanning it; it is used only for the same commit and `scanRevision`, and rebuilt otherwise. The viewer reads it like the out-of-date stamp. Connectors whose imports were added since the base are solid green; ones that lost every import behind them are dotted red; the selected node's connectors keep their own tints and win. The breadcrumb (the second line, under the heading) adds `vs HEAD (static) +2 −1 1 undeclared` (relationships gained, relationships lost, pairs importing each other with no relationship); with no change it says nothing. `undeclared` counts pairs of elements that now import each other with no relationship in the model: they have no connector to color, so run `pathlight diff` to list them. Everything here is a change in static imports, not in what the program does, and it is as old as the last diff.

## Plan

`pathlight plan ELEMENT ...` records the elements an agent is about to work on in `.cache/pathlight/plan.json` (ids only, at most 64, each checked against the model; no ids clears it). The viewer outlines those nodes (and their containers) in purple, adds `plan N` to the breadcrumb, and tags any node with observed activity that is outside the plan `off plan`. It is intent, not observation: it never changes what the activity shows. A plan set more than two hours before the watched session began (or, with no session, more than two hours ago) is not shown, so one left over from earlier work never tags today's.

`pathlight point ELEMENT [--view VIEW] [--journey ID --step N]` is how an agent walking a person through the map (the onboarding skill, `/pathlight:onboarding`) shows them a box: it writes the element (an id from the model, or a box's key from `pathlight brief`), the view if one was named, the journey and step if given (checked against `pathlight flows`), and the time to `.cache/pathlight/point.json`, and nothing else. The viewer reads it twice a second and acts on each pointer once: it selects the element in the view named, else in this view, else in the view that draws it (with a Back entry; no notice, the header's title says where), reveals it, and the header says `← agent: <title>` beside the view's name until the next key. It never changes the column's face (a talk showing stays showing), it is let go while a modal is open or the talk's field has the keys, and nothing afterwards follows the agent: a later pointer is a new, single request. A pointer with a journey and a step turns flow mode on at that step, as `F` would (in the view that draws the journey's steps apart, the step reader open, the `← agent` mark as before), and the viewer stays in it until `esc` or the next pointer; a pointer without a journey leaves a journey the last pointer turned on. A journey written a moment ago is read before the pointer is acted on. A pointer older than the viewer is ignored; `pathlight point` with no element clears the mark. With no viewer open on the workspace the command writes nothing and says so. The skill asks before its first point.

## Review: new dependencies

After each turn the Stop hook runs `pathlight diff`, which lists imports between two components that the model has no relationship for and that the last commit did not have. While any wait for a verdict the header says `▲ N new dependencies · c review` (with an out-of-date check, one badge: `▲ 2 new dependencies · 3 files not in the model · c review`, shortened to `▲ 2 new dependencies · out of date · c review`, then the count alone), and `c` opens the review above the session summary: one row per dependency with an import that makes it. `j`/`k` move, `e` opens that import in the editor at its line, `y` accepts and `n` rejects.

- Rejecting types a request into the agent's pane (the one `pathlight show` was given) to take the dependency out, or to say what bigger change that needs and wait. The row stays, marked `rejected · still in the code`, until a later diff no longer finds it.
- Accepting on a map someone wrote asks the agent to add the relationship to the model. On a drafted map (whose arrows are the imports) nothing is sent: once every new dependency is accepted, and none rejected is still in the code, the map is redrawn from the code, so a redraw never draws one you have not seen.
- Verdicts are kept in `.cache/pathlight/decisions.json`, so a pair is never asked about twice. They record what the person decided, not whether the code is right; the evidence is static imports only.


## Rendering limits

Pass a view ID and use `--workspace` and `--project` to select its model.
Empty views are rejected. Native mode does not support collapsing groups, relationship-link navigation, or editing the model.
The framebuffer follows the terminal viewport rather than allocating a full-diagram canvas. Manual zoom ranges from 12.5% to 800%; fit never goes above 150% and can go below 12.5% to show the whole diagram.
Leaf shapes become filled boxes. Groups become transparent outlines with titles, ordered behind their children.
Labels wrap at cell boundaries and can be clipped on small nodes. Leaf labels hide below six columns or two rows.
Connectors are routed orthogonally around leaf nodes and rasterized onto terminal cells each frame; where no clear route exists they fall back to a plain horizontal-vertical-horizontal path. Unselected connections remain dim; selected ones draw last. Arrowheads occupy the cell next to the target node; dot and diamond markers are drawn as plain arrowheads.
Relationship labels wrap in the scrollable selection panel rather than covering the diagram.
At 100%, coordinates use 16 horizontal pixels per terminal cell. Vertical scale follows the reported terminal cell aspect ratio, falling back to 32 pixels. Nodes and connectors share the camera projection.
Resizing changes the viewport, not the graph layout. Native boxes and scrolling still move at terminal-cell boundaries during easing.
The activity journal is best-effort. Calls without outcomes stop showing as active after 30 seconds, and the activity panel marks them unconfirmed.

## Implementation and verification

Native node boxes, group outlines, and a connector framebuffer share a clipped viewport. Hit testing uses displayed cell bounds, including rounded group titles.
Camera interpolation uses OpenTUI frame callbacks and Three's orthographic camera for the projection. Nothing is drawn by Three or the GPU, so no WebGPU backend is needed. Linux is the verified platform.
LikeC4 parent and child IDs drive hierarchy navigation. Renderable boxes remain siblings with absolute coordinates to avoid nested border offsets.
The isolated tmux tests exercise the flat view and both nested Parcel views, including clicks before and after pan and resize, independent group and child activity, and normal process exit.
The file-browser test requires `nvim` and checks an actual save, resizing during editing, editor failure recovery, file filtering, refresh, and preserved zoomed diagram position.
The Linux editor lifecycle test checks launcher environment forwarding and shutdown of an editor wrapper with a child process.
Navigation tests verify explicit links, rejected empty destinations, restoration of zoomed diagram cells and selection, an unchanged process, and activity updates after returning.
Search tests exercise the ranking (an exact match beats the current view, the current view beats the rest on equal matches, typos, several words), shortcut isolation, pasted text, mouse isolation, resize, going to a view, and closing back to the original diagram cells.
Routing tests check that routes are only horizontal and vertical, avoid nodes in the way, merge into junctions and end in an arrowhead outside the target. The relationship test checks selection colors and long labels that scroll without moving the diagram.
The interactive prototype was manually tried and approved before integration into the CLI. macOS and broader terminal compatibility remain unverified.
